Repair has changed. The older model was mechanical: something broke, replace it.
The newer model asks a second question first, which is what the repair does to
the environment around it.

Elmira and Lora cover what that looks like in practice, including the moment
that surprises most people: there are circumstances where brushing at the wrong
time does more harm than good, and the reason has to do with chemistry rather
than effort.

They also look at the seal between gum and bone, which is the doorway harmful
bacteria use to reach the bloodstream, and at the tools now available for
supporting healing rather than simply closing a hole.
