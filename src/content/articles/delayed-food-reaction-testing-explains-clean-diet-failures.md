Because healthy is a category, not a prescription. A food can be nutritionally
excellent and still be a problem for you specifically. And the reaction you are
having may not show up for two or three days, which makes it nearly impossible
to connect to the meal that caused it.

## Immediate versus delayed

The reactions most people know about are fast. You eat shellfish, your lips
swell, the cause is obvious. That is an IgE allergy, and it announces itself.

Delayed reactions run on a different antibody and a different clock. You eat
something on Monday and feel foggy, achy or bloated on Wednesday. By then you
have eaten thirty other things. Nobody solves that puzzle by paying closer
attention, because attention is not the limiting factor. The delay is.

## Why clean diets make it worse, not better

Here is the trap. When you decide to eat well, you narrow your diet and repeat a
small number of foods constantly. Eggs every morning. Almonds every afternoon.
Spinach in everything. If one of those is a food you react to, you have just
gone from occasional exposure to daily exposure, and you have done it at exactly
the moment you expected to feel better.

This is why people conclude that healthy eating does not work for them. It is
usually not the category. It is one or two items inside it, now being eaten
seven times a week.

## Testing versus elimination

A delayed-reaction panel gives you a list of candidates. It is a screening tool,
not a verdict, and the honest framing is that the results are a starting point
for a structured elimination rather than a final answer. The value is that it
narrows a field of hundreds down to a handful worth actually testing on
yourself.

Elimination alone works too. It just takes months, and most people abandon it
before they get an answer.

If you have been eating well for a year and feeling worse for most of it, a list
of specific candidates is more useful than another round of trying harder.
