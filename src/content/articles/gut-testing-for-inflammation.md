We're looking at specific markers that tell us exactly what's happening at the intestinal level:

### **Zonulin & Occludin**

These proteins regulate the tight junctions between your intestinal cells. Think of them as the gatekeepers of your gut barrier.

When zonulin levels elevate and occludin antibodies appear in your bloodstream, those tight junctions break down, creating increased intestinal permeability — or [leaky gut](https://theurbanmonk.com/leaky-gut-syndrome/).

Research shows zonulin upregulation occurs when the gut is exposed to specific triggers, particularly gluten and bacterial overgrowth⁵.

Occludin antibodies in your blood indicate actual structural breakdown of the gut barrier.

Once these barriers fail, undigested food particles, bacterial fragments, and other inflammatory triggers cross into your bloodstream, activating immune responses throughout your body.

### **LPS (Lipopolysaccharide) Antibodies**

LPS is a component of bacterial cell walls.

When you have elevated antibodies to LPS, it proves bacteria or bacterial fragments have crossed your gut barrier into your bloodstream.

This bacterial translocation triggers the same inflammatory cascade seen in heart disease, diabetes, and autoimmune conditions¹⁰.

### **Candida Antibodies**

Elevated antibodies to Candida indicate yeast overgrowth has transformed into its aggressive form, creating thread-like structures that damage your gut lining while producing inflammatory toxins.

### **IgG Food Sensitivity Testing**

Unlike IgE allergies that cause immediate reactions, IgG antibodies indicate delayed food sensitivities that create chronic inflammation.

Research shows IgG-mediated food sensitivities are significantly associated with intestinal permeability markers like LPS and occludin⁶.

When your gut barrier is compromised, food proteins cross into your bloodstream, triggering IgG production and systemic inflammation⁷.
