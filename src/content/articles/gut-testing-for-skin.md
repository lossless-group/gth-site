## **The Science Behind Why Some Skin Problems Start in Your Gut**

Your gut and your skin are more connected than you might think.

They're both barrier organs — the first line of defense against the outside world.

When one barrier breaks down, the other can follow.

**Here's what research shows happens at a cellular level:**

Studies show that when your gut barrier becomes compromised, inflammatory compounds called lipopolysaccharides (LPS) can leak through.²,¹⁶

These bacterial toxins enter your bloodstream and trigger systemic inflammation.

Your body interprets this as a threat and mounts an immune response — which can manifest as skin inflammation.

Zonulin is the key player here. It's like a gatekeeper protein that controls the tight junctions in your intestinal lining.

When Zonulin gets elevated — triggered by bacterial overgrowth and gluten¹²,¹³ — those tight junctions open up.

Think of it like leaving windows open during a rainstorm — everything that should stay outside comes pouring in.

**The cascade looks like this:**

1.  Trigger exposure (gluten, bacterial overgrowth)
2.  Zonulin release opens intestinal barriers
3.  Inflammatory compounds enter bloodstream
4.  Immune system activates
5.  Systemic inflammation spreads
6.  Skin can erupt as body tries to eliminate toxins

Research studies confirm that intestinal permeability can link to [food sensitivities](https://theurbanmonk.com/food-sensitivities/) — which may create delayed inflammatory reactions that could take 2-72 hours after eating.³,¹⁵

That's why you can't figure out your triggers. You eat eggs on Tuesday and break out on Thursday. [The connection isn't obvious.](/blog/leaky-gut-symptoms)

## **Why Food Sensitivities Might Be Showing Up on Your Face**

Here's something that frustrates many people: ***you're eating "healthy" foods that could be inflaming your gut without you realizing it***.

Food sensitivities aren't the same as food allergies.

Allergies are immediate *(eat peanuts, throat swells)*.

Sensitivities are delayed *(eat gluten Tuesday morning, face breaks out Thursday afternoon)*.

That delay makes them nearly impossible to identify without testing.

Studies in *Frontiers in Nutrition* demonstrate that IgG-mediated food sensitivity can be directly related to increased intestinal barrier permeability.¹⁷

When your gut is leaky, larger food particles pass through that shouldn't. Your immune system may recognize them as threats and create antibodies.

Every time you eat that food, your immune system could react — creating inflammation that might manifest in your skin.

Common culprits:

- Dairy *(even if you're not lactose intolerant)*
- Gluten *(even without celiac disease)*
- Eggs
- Soy
