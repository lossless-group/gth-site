In most cases it is not one thing. Libido sits downstream of sex hormones,
thyroid function, stress hormones, sleep and blood flow, and it drops when any
of those go sideways. Yes, it usually comes back, but not by addressing desire
directly. You have to find which of those inputs slipped.

## The stress hormone takes priority

Your body treats cortisol production as more urgent than sex hormone production,
which is a sensible arrangement for an animal under threat and a miserable one
for a person under deadline. Chronic stress shifts resources accordingly. This
is the most common cause I see, and the one people most resist hearing, because
the fix is not a supplement.

## The part almost nobody mentions

Your gut bacteria participate in hormone regulation directly. A specific group
of them produces an enzyme that determines whether estrogen your liver has
packaged for disposal actually leaves the body or gets unpacked and returned to
circulation.

When that bacterial population is off, the balance goes with it. Too much
recycling and estrogen accumulates. Too little and levels run low. Either
direction affects desire, and neither is visible on a hormone panel alone,
because the panel shows you the level without showing you what is producing it.

## The other usual suspects

Worth ruling out before anything exotic:

- thyroid function, which flattens energy and desire together
- medications, especially SSRIs, hormonal birth control and some blood pressure drugs
- sleep, because most testosterone production happens during it
- blood sugar instability, which drives both fatigue and vascular problems

## Where to start

Get the hormone panel. Then ask the second question the panel cannot answer: why
are these numbers what they are. Stress, sleep and medications account for most
of it. When those are addressed and the numbers still do not move, the gut is
the next place worth looking, and it is looked at far less often than it should
be.
