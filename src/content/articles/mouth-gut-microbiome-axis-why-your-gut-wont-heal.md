If you have done the elimination diet, the probiotics and the protocols and your
gut still will not settle, there is one upstream source almost nobody checks.
Your mouth and your gut are not two separate systems. They are the two ends of a
single continuous tube, and you swallow roughly a liter of saliva a day. Whatever
is living in your mouth is being delivered to your gut on a schedule.

## The delivery is constant

You do not need a dramatic event for oral bacteria to reach your intestine. Every
swallow sends them. In a healthy mouth, stomach acid handles most of what
arrives, and the ones that survive are outnumbered on arrival. In a mouth with
established gum disease, the load is heavier, the species are more aggressive,
and some of them are notably good at surviving the trip.

## Why the downstream work stalls

This is the mechanism behind a frustration I see constantly. Someone does
everything right below the neck and gets partial, temporary results. The
protocol works. It is just being re-seeded every day from above.

The signs that this might be your situation are not subtle once you know to look:

- gums that bleed when you floss or brush
- persistent bad breath that brushing does not fix
- gut symptoms that improve on a strict protocol and return within weeks
- a history of gum treatment, or of avoiding the dentist for years

## What to do with that

Treat the mouth as part of the gut protocol rather than as a separate errand.
That does not mean sterilizing it. Wiping out your oral bacteria with a strong
antiseptic rinse removes the useful species along with the harmful ones and
leaves an empty room that the fast, opportunistic organisms refill first.

It means reducing the harmful load and giving the beneficial species conditions
to hold their ground: pH, saliva flow, and what you feed them.

Before you run another elimination round, it is worth knowing what is actually
living at the top of the tube. That is a measurement, not a guess.
