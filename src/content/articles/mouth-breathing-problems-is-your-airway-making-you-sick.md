One likely answer is that you were not sleeping for all eight of those hours.
If your airway narrows or closes repeatedly through the night, your brain wakes
you enough to restore breathing and then lets you drop back down. You will not
remember any of it. You will just feel like the night did nothing for you.

## Why a dentist is asking about this at all

Because the airway is built out of structures a dentist looks at every visit.
The size and shape of your upper jaw determines how much room your tongue has.
If the jaw developed narrow, the tongue sits further back, and further back is
where it can obstruct. The palate is also the floor of your nasal passage, so a
narrow, high palate means less nasal airway too.

We can often see the pattern before anyone has mentioned sleep. Worn surfaces
from grinding, scalloped edges along the tongue, a narrow arch, crowded teeth
that never had room.

## The mouth breathing loop

Once you are breathing through your mouth, things compound. The mouth dries
overnight, which raises decay risk and shifts the bacterial population toward
the species that like dry and acidic. Nasal breathing, which filters and
humidifies air and generates nitric oxide that helps your blood vessels, is not
happening. In children, chronic mouth breathing influences how the face and jaw
develop, which narrows the airway further.

Signs worth taking seriously:

- waking unrefreshed regardless of hours slept
- dry mouth or a sore throat in the morning
- grinding, or a partner who reports snoring
- afternoon fatigue, brain fog, or difficulty concentrating
- in children, crowded teeth alongside restlessness or trouble focusing

## What to do about it

Start by finding out whether it is happening. A home sleep study is
straightforward and no longer requires a night in a lab. If your dentist has
flagged the structural signs, that finding belongs in front of a physician who
can order one.

A mouth can be perfectly healthy and still be shaped in a way that makes
breathing hard. Those are two separate questions, and both are worth an answer.
