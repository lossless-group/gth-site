Autoimmunity means your immune system has started responding to your own tissue.
Nobody can tell you the full why in your particular case, and anyone who says
otherwise is selling something. What we can say is that genetics set the
conditions, something environmental sets it off, and across a striking number of
autoimmune conditions the gut barrier turns out to be involved, often well
before a diagnosis arrives.

## The barrier has two contradictory jobs

Your intestine has to let nutrients through while keeping nearly everything else
out. It manages this with a single layer of cells held together by junctions
that open and close on demand. When those junctions stay open longer than they
should, partially digested proteins and bacterial fragments reach the immune
tissue waiting on the other side.

Most of your immune system is stationed there, directly behind that wall. It is
not stationed there by accident. That is where the decisions get made about what
is food and what is a threat.

## Where the confusion starts

When the immune system meets the same foreign fragment repeatedly under
inflammatory conditions, it builds a response to it. If that fragment happens to
resemble a protein in your own thyroid, joint lining or nerve sheath, the
response it built does not stop neatly at the border. This is the part of the
story with the most research behind it and still the most argument about how
much it explains.

## What this changes in practice

It shifts the first question. Instead of only asking which drug suppresses the
attack, it becomes reasonable to also ask what is still crossing, and why.
That means looking at what you eat, what you take, how you sleep, how much
stress you carry, and what is living in your gut and in your mouth upstream
of it.

Suppression and repair are not opposites. Plenty of people need both. But only
one of them addresses why the immune system got the message in the first place,
and you cannot work on the barrier without first knowing what state it is in.
